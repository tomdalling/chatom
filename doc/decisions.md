Astu Language Decisions
=======================

The rules of the language, and how it works. Examples are in English -> Astu.

## Grammar

- This is an _analytic_ language. Words do not have affixes. Instead, it relies
  on word order and helper words/particles.

- This is a subject-verb-object (SVO) language, like English.
  - Example: "bird eats fish" -> "berd it fis"

- All verbs are transitive, taking exactly one direct object. For verbs that
  would normally be intransitive, repeat the subject as the direct object.
  - Example: "I sleep" -> "mi slip mi".

- The head noun comes first, and modifier nouns come directly after.
  - Example: "bird egg" -> "eg bird"

- Adjectives are postpositive, coming _after_ the head noun and any modifier
  nouns. Adjectives have no specific order amongst themselves.
  - Example: "small bird egg" -> "eg berd smol"

- There are no articles, per se. There is no "the", "an", or "a" words that
  modify nouns.
  - Example: "the bird eats a fish" -> "berd it fis"

- There are no plurals. To indicate multiple of a noun, use the pronoun "dem" as
  a modifier noun.
  - Example: "eggs" -> "eg dem"
  - This rule was taken from Papiamento, a Carribean creole.

- Repeating an adjective makes it superlative.
  - Example: "huge bird" -> "berd big big"

- The language has Serial Verb Constructions (SVCs): multiple verb phrases can
  be strung together in a single clause with no conjunction between them.
